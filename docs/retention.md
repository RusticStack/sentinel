# Retention and quotas

Every byte of Sentinel's stored content (artifacts, uploads, logs, caches, images, spools) is bounded twice: by **size** (a quota, a budget or a watermark) and, for evidence, by **age** (a retention). The metadata database is the exception: run, job and attempt rows are kept without a bound, see [metadata housekeeping](#metadata-housekeeping). This page is the whole map. Per-class totals (artifacts, uploads, logs and the reported caches) are kept per tenant ([per-class accounting](#per-class-accounting)), every quota is enforced at a hard threshold and warned at a soft one that also tells the tenant's administrators, once per crossing ([thresholds](#thresholds-and-the-hard-limit), [threshold notices](#threshold-notices)), and artifacts are kept under a deployment-defined retention class, pinned by a tenant administrator when they must outlive it ([artifact classes and pins](#artifact-classes-and-pins)). A tenant that states no quota of its own is held to the deployment's default, sized from the disk it fills, rather than to nothing ([the default limit](#the-default-limit)); runner mode's caches on worker hosts are charged to the tenant that caused them and give way at its hard threshold ([actions cache](actions-cache.md#what-the-cache-is-charged-to)). The disk admission gate, object reclamation and the orphan sweep it builds on are in [storage](storage.md#disk-admission-quotas-and-reclamation).

## What is bounded, and by what

| Kind | Where | Size bound | Age bound | Collected by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Artifacts | controller objects | run budget, repository quota (counted at the artifact's class weight), tenant quota, deployment cap | the pipeline's `retain`, shortened to its class's maximum age and the effective artifact retention; a pin holds it until unpinned | artifact sweep, then reference-safe reclaim ([storage](storage.md#disk-admission-quotas-and-reclamation)) |
| Uploads | controller objects | tenant quota, deployment cap; 4 GiB per session | session TTL ≤ 24 h; aborted rows kept 7 days | upload sweep; aborted-row purge |
| Attempt logs | controller `logs/` | `attempt_log_bytes` per attempt; counted in repository, tenant and deployment usage | the effective log retention | database-driven expiry, then the directory sweep |
| External S3 copy ([s3](s3.md)) | the bucket | the backlog budget closes admission; `local_bytes` bounds local copies of replicated objects | follows the local record: deleted after reclamation or log expiry | the replicator |
| Remote cache store | controller `remote-cache/` | `[remote_cache] budget_bytes` and the owning tenant's hard threshold, least recently served first | — | its own reclamation thread, every 10 min; a tenant-scoped eviction when that tenant reaches its hard threshold ([cache](cache.md#remote-hydration)) |
| Local cache | worker `cache/` | `cache_budget_bytes`, least recently used, leased entries skipped | 14 days idle | the cache GC after every attempt ([cache](cache.md)) |
| Git mirrors | worker `mirrors/` | `mirror_budget_bytes`, least recently written, leased mirrors skipped | 14 days idle | the mirror sweep, same cadence ([mirrors](mirrors.md)) |
| Container images | worker image stores | `image_budget_bytes` per store, least recently pulled | — | the image sweep, at most every minute (below) |
| Log spools | worker `spool/` | `spool_quota_bytes`, `spool_reserve_bytes` | until acknowledged | spool delivery ([logs](logs.md)) |
| Everything on the controller disk | data file system | reserve, low/high watermarks, log floor | — | admission closes before the disk fills ([storage](storage.md#disk-admission-quotas-and-reclamation)) |

## Policy at each level

The deployment's `[storage]` configuration ([configuration](configuration.md)) sets the defaults. A **storage policy** — one `storage_policies` row — overrides them for one tenant or one repository; a field left unset inherits:

| Level | Set by | Quota | Thresholds | Log retention | Artifact retention (the longest a pipeline may ask for) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deployment | `[storage]`: `quota_bytes`, `tenant_quota_bytes`, `personal_quota_bytes`, `soft_percent`, `hard_percent`, `log_retention_secs`, `artifact_retention_secs`, `run_artifact_bytes`, `attempt_log_bytes` | cap on every tenant together, plus the default an organization and a personal namespace inherit ([the default limit](#the-default-limit)) | 80 % warned, 100 % enforced of each tenant's quota | 14 days | 90 days |
| Tenant | platform administration: `PUT /admin/tenants/{slug}/storage`, `admin tenant storage` | its own cap, or an explicit unlimited (0) | lower than the deployment's, never higher | any value 1 h – 366 d | any value 1 h – 366 d |
| Repository | the tenant's administration: `PUT /tenants/{slug}/repos/{name}/storage`, `admin tenant storage --repo` | a cap inside the tenant's (never above it) | never its own: the line is the tenant's | only shorter than the tenant's | only shorter than the tenant's |
| Run | configuration | `run_artifact_bytes` of artifacts per run | — | — |

Retention only narrows going down: a repository's effective retention is the smaller of its own and its tenant's, so a tenant administrator can shorten how long a repository's evidence is kept but never keep it past what the platform allows the tenant. If the tenant's retention is later lowered below a repository's, the tenant's wins.

**What counts.** A tenant's usage is its committed objects, the declared length of its open uploads, its stamped log bytes, and the bytes its runner-mode caches hold on the worker hosts its jobs ran on. A repository's is the logical bytes of its captured artifacts, each multiplied by its class's quota weight (before the tenant-wide deduplication, which is the tenant's saving, not the repository's), and its logs. The tenant's own totals stay physical and unweighted, because a deduplicated object cannot carry several classes' weights. The deployment's is every tenant's. The same totals split by class are under [per-class accounting](#per-class-accounting).

**What is refused.** An object or upload that would take a tenant past its hard threshold, or the deployment past its cap, is `quota_exceeded` (403). An artifact whose repository is at its quota, or whose run is at `run_artifact_bytes`, is refused to the worker (`TooLarge`) when it begins, as the disk watermarks already did. The run budget is also charged file by file while the artifact streams (what other artifacts of the run hold counts, and a refused one gives its share back), and the repository quota is checked again in the commit transaction with the artifact's real size, so an artifact whose size the begin check could not know never takes a repository past its quota: it is refused `TooLarge`, its staged objects are discarded and a failed row records it. A failed artifact holds no bytes against the repository: its row carries zero bytes whatever it had staged before the refusal. An artifact that would pass the tenant's hard threshold is refused the same way, after the tenant's caches have been evicted, and the reason is recorded on the row ([thresholds](#thresholds-and-the-hard-limit)). **Logs are counted, never refused for quota**: a log is the evidence of what ran, and refusing it would hide a failure. They are bounded instead by `attempt_log_bytes` and their retention, and a disk below the log floor still refuses them ([storage](storage.md#disk-admission-quotas-and-reclamation)).

## Thresholds and the hard limit

A tenant's quota is enforced at a **hard** threshold and warned at a **soft** one, both as percentages of the tenant's effective quota, so one policy serves every quota size. The deployment sets the defaults, `soft_percent = 80` and `hard_percent = 100` of the quota; a **tenant** narrows either one through its storage policy (`PUT /admin/tenants/{slug}/storage`, `admin tenant storage --soft-percent/--hard-percent`) and can never widen it past the deployment's. A **repository** row never carries a threshold: the line a tenant is measured against is the tenant's, and a repository narrows only its own quota and retentions. Lowering the deployment's later tightens every tenant that never set its own, and an effective soft threshold is never above the effective hard one.

The byte counts an operator sees (`soft_limit_bytes`, `hard_limit_bytes`) are the first byte at which the threshold is reached, rounded up, so a displayed figure and the comparison the controller makes are the same boundary. An unlimited tenant (quota 0) has no threshold and is always `ok`.

**At the soft threshold nothing is refused.** It is a state — `ok`, `soft` or `hard` — that `GET /tenants/{slug}/storage` reports (`thresholds.state`) and that `sentinel_storage_tenants{state}` counts across the deployment without a tenant label ([monitoring](monitoring.md)). Uploads, artifacts and logs behave exactly as below it.

**At the hard threshold,** in this order:

1. The tenant's caches give way. Every remote-cache bundle of the tenant's repositories leaves the controller's store, and runner-mode `actions_cache` entries take the same place in the order ([cache](cache.md#remote-hydration)); the class arrives before artifacts do.
2. An artifact publication that would pass the threshold is then refused. The worker is answered `quota_exceeded` (`ArtifactCode::QuotaExceeded`, code 9; an older worker reads it as `stale` and fails the artifact rather than storing it), its staged objects are discarded, and the artifact's row records the refusal in `failure`: the bytes stored, the hard threshold and the quota it is a percentage of, the artifact's own bytes, and the three largest classes. That text is what `GET /runs/{id}/artifacts` returns and what the attempt's failure names when the artifact was `required`.
3. Nothing else stops. **Logs stay counted and are never refused** for a threshold: they are the evidence of what ran. **Jobs keep running**: placement, leases, log frames, reports and cancellation are untouched, and a tenant at its hard threshold only loses artifacts and caches.

The threshold is the enforced bound everywhere the quota was: an upload whose declared length would pass it is `quota_exceeded` (403), an object committed past it is discarded. Both count what the standing counts, the reported runner-mode caches included, so an upload is refused at the line the storage page draws; a refused upload makes the tenant's caches give way first (every worker holding them is asked, idle ones included), and its retry is admitted once the workers reported what they removed. The deployment-wide cap (`quota_bytes`) is still enforced at 100 % of itself. The refusal is decided in the single writer transaction that commits the artifact, so concurrent publications cannot both pass a limit only one of them fits; the eviction runs outside it, since a filesystem walk must never hold up the writer.

## Threshold notices

A crossing of the soft threshold is told **once**, and told again only after usage has come back down. The state that makes that true is one row in `storage_notices` per tenant currently over the line, written in the same single-writer transaction that queues the notice itself; the row stands for "told", so a controller that restarts re-reads it and sends nothing twice, and it is **deleted** when usage falls back under the re-arm line, which arms the next crossing. Nothing on a request path looks for a crossing: usage is written by triggers from artifact commits, uploads, log stamps and their expiry, so the API's own pass looks once every 60 seconds, does one load and no query at all when nothing has been committed since it last looked, and judges each tenant against the same `retention::standing` the storage page reports, so a notice and the page beside it cannot disagree about where the line is.

**Who is told, and how.** The tenant's administrators as the mail outbox reaches them: the members with the admin role that are people with a **verified** address (never a service account, at most 32 per crossing). When delivery is configured (`[mail]` and a master key) one message is queued for each of them, its kind is `3` (`storage-notice`), and it is sealed and expires like any other queued message — a notice that could not be delivered within a day is given up rather than mailed late. Otherwise, or when no administrator has a verified address, the notice is **in place**: it is reported by `GET /tenants/{slug}/storage` as `notice` (`threshold_percent`, `used_bytes`, `limit_bytes`, `delivery` `mail` or `in_app`, `notified_ms`, `null` when no crossing stands) on the storage page's own report. Either way one audit row records it, event 99 `storage-threshold-notified`, with the tenant's slug and the percentage used and no address in the detail.

**The re-arm line** is 75 % of the quota, so a tenant oscillating around the threshold — 82 %, 76 %, 83 %, 74 % — is told twice at most, once per crossing downward past the line and back up. A tenant whose own soft threshold is *below* 75 % narrows the line to just under its threshold, because that band would otherwise invert and tell its administrators on every pass. The notice says when it will speak again, so nobody waits for a message that will not come.

**What a notice is not.** It refuses nothing and evicts nothing: it reports the soft threshold the tenant has already crossed, and the hard threshold's eviction and refusal ([above](#thresholds-and-the-hard-limit)) are the only consequences of being over a limit. A tenant with no quota has no threshold and is never told.

## Per-class accounting

A tenant's storage is split by what holds it, in six classes. The split is kept beside the quota's own totals, on the same row (`tenant_usage`), so the numbers the quota enforces and the numbers a tenant is shown cannot disagree.

| Class | What it counts | Kept by | Quota |
|---|---|---|---|
| `artifacts` | the bytes of objects at least one artifact manifest references, once each however many manifests name them (native and runner-mode artifacts share the one store) | triggers on `objects` and `manifest_refs`, in the transaction that writes the row | counted and enforced: tenant, repository and run limits as above |
| `uploads` | the declared length of open upload sessions plus the objects no manifest references (a committed upload nobody has named in an artifact, an object whose last manifest was retired and that waits for reclamation) | triggers on `uploads`, `objects` and `manifest_refs` | counted and enforced |
| `logs` | the stamped bytes of unexpired attempt logs | the log triggers of [log expiry](#log-expiry-is-decided-in-the-database) | counted, never refused |
| `remote_cache` | the bundles the controller's remote cache store keeps, attributed to the tenant that owns each bundle's repository | the store's reclamation pass, every 10 minutes | **reported**, and given up first: the store has its own budget (`[remote_cache] budget_bytes`) and does not count toward the tenant quota, but at the tenant's hard threshold every bundle of its repositories is evicted ([thresholds](#thresholds-and-the-hard-limit)) |
| `actions_cache` | runner mode's cache entries on worker hosts: what each worker's `actions/cache` tree holds for each of the tenant's repositories, as that worker's own walk measured it | the writer transaction that folds a worker's cache report into `worker_actions_cache`, summed per tenant and then per repository owner | counted and enforced: it is what lets the hard threshold see the entries at all, and it is the first class to give way ([actions cache](actions-cache.md#what-the-cache-is-charged-to)) |
| `worker_cache` | the local cache bytes each live worker of the tenant's dedicated pools last reported in its availability | the trigger that follows a worker's profile report (and its revocation) | **reported, never enforced**: a worker's cache is bounded by its own budget on its own disk. A shared pool's workers belong to no tenant and are counted for none |

`artifact_bytes + upload_bytes` is always the quota's object bytes (`bytes`) and `log_bytes` is the quota's log bytes, so what the quota counts is exactly artifacts + uploads + logs + `actions_cache_bytes`; `remote_cache` and `worker_cache` are never part of it — a remote bundle is a second copy of objects the tenant already paid for, and a worker's local cache is bounded by that worker's own budget. The classes of the artifact store are physical, after the tenant-wide deduplication, which is what the disk and the quota hold; a repository's own usage ([above](#policy-at-each-level)) stays the logical bytes of its artifacts. A class is never negative: each new column carries a `CHECK`, and the triggers add and subtract the same value a row contributed.

**Exact after a crash.** The three database-held classes change in the transaction that writes the underlying fact, by triggers whose cost does not depend on how much the tenant already holds (an object entering or leaving, the first or last manifest edge to it, a session opening, sealing or aborting: one index probe and one update of the tenant's row; `a_class_update_costs_the_same_however_much_the_tenant_already_holds` measures the steps at 4 and at 600 rows). There is no rescan on a write path and nothing to repair after a crash: a transaction that did not commit changed no class, and one that did committed its classes with it. `classes::recount` is the audit: it adds every class up from the rows themselves and the tests compare it with the kept totals after every step of a mixed sequence, after a copy of the files taken between an operation and the pass that follows it, and after the migration's backfill. The reported classes are published whole by their passes and replace the previous figure; a crash between a reclamation and its report leaves the previous figure until the next pass. A remote cache walk that stopped early (it is bounded by entries) or could not read a directory reports nothing, so a partial walk never shows a tenant less than it holds; a tenant that held bytes and is absent from a complete walk is set to zero, in batches of 256.

**Daily snapshots.** The maintenance pass records each tenant's classes once per UTC day in `storage_class_snapshots` (`(tenant_id, day)` with the six totals and the wall-clock time taken, which is provenance only). The first pass of a day takes it: 256 tenants per writer transaction, up to 16 transactions per pass, and a day with more tenants than that resumes after its last key at the next pass, as the other sweeps continue a full batch. A pass a running backup keeps out (it holds the maintenance lock) did not happen and is retried at the next tick. A day is taken once: an existing row is kept, so a restart repeats no work that matters, and a day already complete costs nothing until the date changes. A tenant keeps at most 90 rows: the table's own trigger removes everything older than a tenant's newest 90 whatever path inserted the row, so the 91st day drops the oldest and one tenant's rows never prune another's. The bound is 90 snapshots, not 90 calendar days: a controller that was stopped for a week has fewer days to show, not more. Only tenants with a usage row are snapshotted.

**Metrics.** `sentinel_storage_class_bytes{class}` carries the six totals of every tenant together, without a tenant label, so a scrape names nobody and the series count is fixed ([monitoring](monitoring.md)). There is no setting that adds a tenant label; per-tenant figures and their trend are read from the store (`classes::tenant`, `classes::snapshots`, `classes::largest`, `classes::expiring`) and are what the tenant's own page shows ([below](#what-the-storage-pages-show)).

## What the storage pages show

A tenant administrator sees its own tenant and nothing else: `GET /tenants/{slug}/storage` and **Tenant admin → Storage**.

| Part | What it answers | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Usage against the quota | stored bytes and the percentage of the effective quota used, with `thresholds.state` (`ok`, `soft`, `hard`) and both lines in bytes and percent, so the page and a refusal are measured by the same numbers | `retention::standing`, `retention::limit_bytes` |
| By class | the six class totals, each with its share of what is stored and whether the quota counts it; the caches are marked as reported | `classes::tenant` |
| By repository | each repository's artifacts and logs, its quota and its retentions, editable in place | `retention::repos` |
| The trend | one bar per recorded day, newest last, over the last `days` snapshots (30 by default, at most the 90 kept) | `classes::snapshots` |
| The largest artifacts | the ten heaviest captured artifacts, heaviest first, each with its repository, class and deadline | `classes::largest` |
| What expires within 7 days | the artifact bytes and count and the log bytes and count due in the window, plus the ten artifacts expiring soonest (a pinned artifact is in neither, because the sweep keeps it) | `classes::expiring` |
| The notice | the standing soft-threshold crossing and how it was delivered | `notices::notice` |

A platform administrator sees every tenant at once: **Platform → Tenants** lists each with `used_percent`, `state` and both limit bytes beside the quota field, and the same dialog sets a tenant's overrides — its quota, its retentions and its two thresholds, each blank meaning inherit the deployment's and a threshold never above it. The page reads them from `retention::standing` per row, so a percentage in the list and the state `sentinel_storage_tenants` counts are the same figure.

**What a page costs.** Each part is one bounded read: a keyed row per tenant, a top-N sort of the tenant's own artifacts (the one sort the metadata schema cannot index for), and two range reads over the expiry indexes — one for artifacts due inside the window, one for logs. No request recounts anything, and none of them touches another tenant's rows except the log window, whose partial index is keyed by deadline and filters the tenant out of the rows it reads.

**What is not proven here.** The store, the cache sweep's per-repository totals and the maintenance pass's snapshots run in the tests below. The controller's remote cache reclamation thread calling the report is wired but not driven end to end by a test, and no figure from a live host is claimed. A worker's runner-mode cache report is driven over a real link, from the report through the fold, the hard state and the `ActionsEvict` it is answered with (`crates/sentinel-link/tests/runner_mode.rs`); what no test covers is the walk of a cache tree a real job filled, which is the worker's own directory read.

## Log expiry is decided in the database

A log's deadline and its removal are recorded in the database, not read from file ages:

1. **Stamp.** Once an attempt is released, the maintenance pass reads its log's size from disk (off the writer) and stamps the row: `log_bytes` (added to the tenant's and repository's usage by trigger) and `log_expires_ms` — the release time plus the effective retention of its repository. Up to 16 batches of 256 per pass, oldest release first, so a backlog catches up quickly.
2. **Expire.** Rows past their deadline (`attempts_log_expiry`) get `log_expired_ms` and `log_bytes = 0` in one transaction — the usage drops with it — re-checking the deadline, so a retention raised meanwhile keeps the log. The files are deleted after that commits. Expiry also catches up in up to 16 batches of 256 per pass, and so do the artifact sweep, object reclaim and the aborted-upload purge: a pass whose batch came back full goes on with the next, one writer transaction per batch, so a deployment finishing more than a few thousand attempts an hour still expires them as fast as they arrive. A pass that a running backup keeps out (it holds the maintenance lock) did not happen and is retried at the next tick, not after a whole `sweep_interval`. A row whose id cannot be decoded (outside damage; every id column is a 16-byte blob the store wrote) is passed over, counted in `sentinel_maintenance_rows_skipped_total` and logged as `maintenance_rows_skipped` with its table and key, and the rows behind it are still served. The count rises each time a batch meets the row, not once per row.
3. **Sweep.** The bounded directory walk (`LogStore::sweep_dirs`) deletes what the rows no longer account for: a directory whose row says expired (a crash between commit and delete) or that no attempt row names at all. Open writers are never touched.

An expired log never comes back: the `attempt_update` trigger refuses to clear `log_expired_ms`, the worker link refuses late frames for it, and the API answers its page and search at once with `expired_ms` and no frames; the web interface says the log was removed by retention and when.

**Changing a policy applies to what is stored.** Setting a tenant's or repository's policy re-stamps the unexpired logs it covers to their new deadline (release plus the new retention — shorter or longer) and shortens artifact deadlines past the new artifact retention. The policy row and the first 512 rows commit in one transaction (`retention::set`); the rest is re-applied by `retention::finish_reapply`, in keyset order by row id and one write per 512 of the tenant's rows (logs first, then artifacts) read through the keyed indexes `attempts_log_reapply` and `artifacts_reapply` (migration 049), so what other tenants hold is never scanned, a policy change on a large tenant never holds the writer for its size, and each step is idempotent (the plans are described under [reapply plans](#reapply-plans)). The API and `sentinel admin tenant storage` run it before they answer; a crash between the set and the end of the re-stamping leaves the remaining rows on their previous deadlines until the policy is set again. A lowered retention therefore frees space on the next maintenance pass, not only for new runs. An artifact deadline is never extended: what a pipeline asked for is not recorded beyond its deadline.

## Artifact classes and pins

Every artifact is stored under a **class** the deployment defines: a maximum age it is kept for, whatever its pipeline's `retain` asks, and a quota weight. Four defaults exist (`debug`, `standard`, `evidence`, `release`), and `[storage.classes]` replaces them ([configuration](configuration.md)); a class's `max_age_secs` is at most the deployment's `artifact_retention_secs`, and its `weight` is 1 to 100.

| Class | Maximum age | Weight | Meant for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `debug` | 7 days | 1 | diagnostics and dumps nobody keeps |
| `standard` | 30 days | 1 | the default; an artifact that names no class takes `artifact_default_class` (`standard`) |
| `evidence` | 90 days | 1 | reports and logs a person may need after a failure |
| `release` | 90 days | 2 | built output worth keeping longest, and charged double against the repository quota |

**What is enforced at insert.** `artifacts.class` holds the declaration's class, or the deployment's default class, and `artifacts.charged_bytes` its bytes times that class's weight. The `artifacts_retention_cap` trigger shortens a new row's `retain_until_ms` to the smaller of its class's maximum age and the effective artifact retention its repository inherits, whatever code path inserts the row (a captured artifact, a failed one, the terminal publication's missing ones). A name the deployment does not define is refused before a run exists (intake settles it `failed:artifact_class_unknown`, and a manually dispatched pipeline is `invalid_request`), and the `artifacts_class_known` trigger refuses one inserted by any other path. The repository quota counts `charged_bytes`, so a `release` artifact of 1 GiB holds 2 GiB of its repository's quota; the tenant's own totals stay physical and unweighted, because a deduplicated object cannot carry several classes' weights.

**Pins.** A tenant administrator pins an artifact (`PUT /api/v1/runs/{run}/artifacts/{arf}/pin`, `DELETE` to unpin) and it is kept until unpinned: the artifact sweep skips a pinned row, the archive download ignores its past deadline, and its bytes stay in the tenant's, repository's and deployment's usage the whole time, because the row is still there. `pinned_ms` records when it was pinned; pinning twice keeps the first time. Unpinning clears the column and puts the stored deadline back into effect, so a row whose deadline has passed is collected by the next sweep. A re-application skips pinned rows: their deadlines are not the deployment's to re-stamp while they are held. Only the tenant's administrator may pin: it is a retention decision, not an operator action.

**Changing a class applies to what is stored.** A class whose maximum age or weight changes is re-applied to its own artifacts only: `retention::class_reapply_step` walks the class's rows in keyset order through `artifacts_class_reapply` (migration 68), re-stamping `retain_until_ms` down to the new ceiling and rewriting `charged_bytes` at the new weight, one writer transaction per 512 rows, with `repo_usage` adjusted by the difference as each row changes. The controller runs it at start, after writing the class map, before it serves. A removed class keeps the artifacts already stored under it (their deadlines stand, and a policy change still re-stamps them by the deployment's retention alone) but refuses new ones under that name. Raising a class's maximum age never extends an artifact already stored, exactly as raising a retention does not ([below](#log-expiry-is-decided-in-the-database)).

**What is not proven here.** Class weights are enforced for the repository quota only; the tenant and deployment quotas count physical bytes with no weight, and the run budget is unchanged. No test measures a re-application on a large artifact table; the plan and the bounded batch are, as for [reapply plans](#reapply-plans).

### Reapply plans

A step is two statements built by `retention::reapply_sql`: the cursor bound (`SELECT id ... ORDER BY id LIMIT 1 OFFSET 511`) and the update confined to `(after, bound]`. Both name their index with `INDEXED BY`, which SQLite accepts on the partial index `attempts_log_reapply` as on `artifacts_reapply`, and both plan as `SEARCH ... (tenant_id=? AND id>? ...)`, with no `SCAN` of the data tables, no sort and no temporary set. A repository scope is applied inside that range: for logs a column filter, for artifacts a primary-key probe of the artifact's run (`EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM runs WHERE id = artifacts.run_id AND ...)`). It was an `IN (SELECT id FROM runs ...)` subquery, which SQLite built into a set of every run of the repository on every batch, so a repository-scoped step cost the repository's runs and not its batch; no behavior changes, the cursor and the result are the same. The rows a step reads are therefore at most one batch of the tenant's candidate rows. A repository-scoped pass still walks the tenant's candidate rows to the end, because the cursor is the tenant's row order, but one write per batch at a time and linear in them once. No `(tenant_id, repo_id, id)` index is added: a policy change is a rare administrative act, and the extra index would cost every attempt write. `tests/retention.rs` asserts the plans and that the work of a two-row batch is the same at 4 and at 60 rows, scoped or not; a reapply measurement on a large table is still unmeasured.

A class re-application has its own two statements (`retention::class_reapply_sql`): the cursor bound and the update are confined to one class through `artifacts_class_reapply`, plan as `SEARCH artifacts USING INDEX artifacts_class_reapply (class=? AND id>?)` with one primary-key probe of the run each row reads, and skip pinned rows. No other class's rows are read, so changing `debug` costs the `debug` artifacts and nothing else.

A pinned row is outside both re-applications: the artifact filters of the policy re-application and the class pass both require `pinned_ms IS NULL`.

**The artifact cap is a trigger.** The controller writes the deployment's limits to `storage_defaults` and its class map to `artifact_classes` at every start, and an `artifacts` insert trigger shortens a new row's `retain_until_ms` to the smaller of its class's maximum age and the effective artifact retention — whatever code path inserts it (a captured artifact, a failed one, the terminal publication's missing ones). A class change is re-applied to the rows it covers by its own bounded pass ([artifact classes and pins](#artifact-classes-and-pins)).

## Sizes chosen from the hardware

Fixed sizes fit one disk badly: 1 GiB of reserve is plenty on 50 GB and a rounding error on 2 TB, where one busy hour of logs outruns it. Unset sizes are therefore derived from the data file system (`statvfs` total) at start and logged (`storage_configured` on the controller, `worker_budgets` on a worker):

| Setting | Default | Clamp |
|---|---|---|
| reserve | a 64th of the disk | 1–16 GiB |
| low watermark (free above the reserve) | a 32nd | 2–32 GiB |
| high watermark | twice low | — |
| log floor | an eighth of the reserve | — |
| worker cache | a fifth | 1–50 GiB |
| worker mirrors | a tenth | 1–50 GiB |
| worker images (per store) | a fifth | 2–50 GiB |
| controller remote cache store | a tenth | 1–50 GiB |
| default quota, organization tenant | a 20th | 8–50 GiB |
| default quota, personal namespace | a 100th | 2–10 GiB |

The reference host (the verification VPS: 12 vCPU AMD EPYC 9645, 31 GiB, ext4 on a virtio disk) measures **1,080,795,422,720 bytes** (263,866,070 blocks of 4 KiB), which gives a 15.7 GiB reserve, closing at 31.5 GiB free above it and reopening at 63 GiB, a 2.0 GiB log floor, and 50 GiB for each worker and remote store. On 100 GiB a worker keeps 20 GiB of cache, 10 of mirrors and 20 of images, leaving half the disk to workspaces and spools. The worker stores together take at most half the disk by default. The same figure sizes the two per-tenant defaults below, which land on their ceilings there: 50 GiB for an organization and 10 GiB for a personal namespace.

**The reserve grows with the metadata database.** The reserve in force is the configured (or sized) one or twice the database's size on disk (main file plus write-ahead log), whichever is larger: room for the WAL to grow to the database's size before a checkpoint and for the database itself to keep growing, measured on every maintenance pass. `GET /admin/storage` and `admin objects status` report it.

Retention defaults (14 days of logs, 90 of artifacts) are policy, not hardware; 90 days caps what a pipeline's `retain` may ask for.

## The default limit

A tenant that states no quota of its own is not unlimited: it inherits the deployment's default, sized from the data file system at start like every other unset size. A personal namespace answers to its own, because it is one person's space rather than a team's evidence.

| Namespace | Key | Default | Floor | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Organization | `[storage] tenant_quota_bytes` | a 20th of the data file system | 8 GiB | 50 GiB |
| Personal | `[storage] personal_quota_bytes` | a 100th of it | 2 GiB | 10 GiB |

**The arithmetic, on the reference host.** Its data file system measures 1,080,795,422,720 bytes, the figure this page's other sizes are derived from. A twentieth of it is 54,039,771,136 bytes and a hundredth 10,807,954,227, both above their ceilings, so an organization tenant there defaults to 50 GiB and a personal namespace to 10 GiB. Fifty GiB is three runs' whole `run_artifact_bytes` budget (3 × 16 GiB) with room for fourteen days of logs, so a tenant running full-size pipelines is not capped before its own evidence expires; a twentieth is also about the share of the disk one of nineteen such tenants may hold before the reserve and the low watermark (15.7 GiB and 31.5 GiB there, 47 GiB together) close admission rather than the quota. The floor keeps a small disk honest — 1 GiB per tenant on a 32 GiB box would let one tenant take it — and the ceiling stops a 4 TB disk from handing one tenant more than any deployment has needed.

**What the tree does not measure.** No committed record measures a pilot's storage footprint, so the default is sized from the disk and from the run budget rather than from measured usage; the largest artifact the records carry is a 48 MiB archive test entry and a 12.6 MB digest-first transfer, and no deployment has reported a tenant's stored bytes. An operator on a different host sets `tenant_quota_bytes` and `personal_quota_bytes` to what that deployment will hold.

**Precedence is unchanged: deployment, then tenant, then repository.** A tenant's own quota row wins over either default, and a repository's quota still narrows its tenant's and never lifts it. A field the tenant leaves unset inherits the default for its namespace kind, so turning one organization into a capped one is a single write on the deployment, and no policy row exists to keep.

**Unlimited is explicit, and it is the platform's.** `0` on a tenant's policy row means no ceiling at all: no threshold, always `ok`, nothing ever refused. Only a platform administrator can state it — `PUT /admin/tenants/{slug}/storage` with `{"quota_bytes": 0}`, or `admin tenant storage --bytes 0` — because the tenant-level storage row is platform administration already ([authorization](authorization.md)); a repository row refuses it (`invalid_request`), since a repository narrows and never lifts. A deployment may state `0` in `[storage]` to give every tenant that inherits from it no default at all. Clearing a tenant's own unlimited (`--clear`) puts the deployment's default back in force.

**The default applies to what is already stored.** It is read at every check rather than stamped on a row, so a tenant with no policy row is measured against it on its next upload, artifact publication or storage report, and a tenant already past it is `hard` from that moment: its caches are evicted at the next hard-threshold pass and its artifact publications are refused until it drops below the line or a platform administrator lifts it. Nothing is re-applied and nothing is recalculated, which is why the upgrade preflight lists the tenants a new default caps before an operator runs it ([upgrades](upgrades.md#before-upgrading)).

## Container images

A worker's images live in its shared rootless store and one private store per tenant ([executor](executor.md)). At most once a minute after an attempt, each store over `image_budget_bytes` gives up images least recently pulled first:

- only images no container uses (`podman images` reports the count; removal is never forced, so podman itself refuses one in use — `tests/podman.rs::an_image_a_container_holds_is_never_reclaimed` proves it against the real runtime);
- never a digest pulled for use in the last ten minutes (an attempt pulls, then creates its container) or one being pulled;
- at most 16 per store per pass.

The size is podman's per-image figure; layers shared between images count for each, so a store's sum errs high and it is trimmed early rather than late.

## Metadata housekeeping

- Aborted upload sessions are deleted a week after they expired; committed ones stay, because they authorize reads of the object they produced ([storage](storage.md#resumable-uploads-reads-and-materialization)).
- Run, job and attempt rows, compiled run specifications and the audit trails are kept **without an age or size bound**: they are the record of what ran and who did what, the audit tables refuse deletes by trigger, run specifications are immutable and reruns reuse them. Only the metadata reserve tracks their growth; no quota counts them and no setting prunes them. This is a documented limit, not a bound: a pruning policy (summarize, then drop terminal run detail after N days, keep the audit) needs a compatibility design of its own and is not planned.

  **Sizing.** The backup drill measured about 2.1 KiB of metadata per run (20,000 runs came to 41.6 MiB, [backup](backup.md#recovery-objectives)), so one run a second is about 60 to 66 GiB of database a year and one thousand runs a day is about 0.75 GiB a year. The database size drives the snapshot time of every backup (`VACUUM INTO` copies it whole), the restore time and the metadata reserve that admission holds back (twice the database, so the data disk needs that much more free space as it grows). Alert on `sentinel_storage_metadata_bytes` against the disk you can afford, size the data disk and the backup target for the database you expect in a year, and plan a move to a larger disk before the reserve reaches the low watermark.

## Operating it

- `GET /admin/storage` (platform) and the web interface's **Platform → Storage**: the disk, the metadata database, the watermarks and reserve in force, whether admission is open, everything tenants store against the deployment's limits, and `removed` — the artifacts, logs, objects and bytes the maintenance pass has taken away since start, so a lowered retention says what it freed instead of the figure only dropping. The pass logs `storage_removed` with the same four numbers.
- `GET /tenants/{slug}/storage` (tenant administration) and **Tenant admin → Storage**: the usage by class and by repository against the quota and its thresholds, the trend, the largest artifacts and what expires within 7 days, with each repository's policy editable in place. **Platform → Tenants** lists the tenants by the percentage of their limit used and sets a tenant's quota, retentions and thresholds ([above](#what-the-storage-pages-show)).
- Host-locally, beside a stopped controller: `sentinel admin tenant storage --tenant SLUG [--repo NAME] [--quota BYTES] [--soft-percent 70] [--hard-percent 90] [--log-retention 14d] [--artifact-retention 30d] [--inherit quota|log-retention|artifact-retention|thresholds|all]` shows or changes a policy (validated against the limits the controller last started with); `admin objects status` reports free and total bytes, the metadata database and the reserve it needs, the watermarks this disk sizes, and per-tenant usage.
