# Logs: frames, spool, acknowledgement and redaction (W05, D04)

Implemented in `sentinel-protocol::logs` (the frame and record encoding), `sentinel-worker::{redact, spool, logpipe}` (the worker side), `sentinel-store::logs` (the controller's segmented log files), the `Log`/`LogEnd`/`LogAck`/`LogEndAck`/`LogRefused` messages of the [worker link](worker-link.md), and `sentinel admin logs`.

## The path a byte takes

1. **Read.** The step's stdout and stderr are read from the `podman exec` pipes in chunks of at most 8 KiB by the reader threads (`process::Sink`). The 64 KiB tails kept for diagnostics are unchanged.
2. **Redact.** Each chunk passes through the attempt's `Redactor` before anything else sees it. Registered values (`Executor::register_secret(attempt, value)`, per attempt, taking effect at once also while the attempt runs; nothing registered for one attempt touches another's output) are replaced by `***`. A value split across two reads is still caught: the redactor holds back exactly the bytes that are the beginning of some secret until the next chunk (or the end of the step) settles them — also when a shorter secret already matches but a longer one may still be arriving — and emits everything else at once. Held-back bytes are released as the step that printed them when it ends. Every non-empty value is registered (`MIN_SECRET_BYTES` is 1), so a short value can cause noisy redaction. Registration also adds every standard JSON escaping of the value and, for a multi-line value, every line holding anything but ASCII punctuation and whitespace, whatever its length (raw and escaped), so structured producers that escape or split a value per line are redacted too ([secrets](secrets.md)); only punctuation-only lines such as `}` stay visible. Duplicates are dropped when the automata are rebuilt, so this costs nothing per output byte. Matching is leftmost-longest and costs O(output) whatever the number or length of the values (P10D-10): two Aho–Corasick automata are built per registration set, one over the values reversed (the longest value starting at each position, in one backward pass) and one over the values as they are (the longest held tail); before, a long near-miss value cost up to its length in comparisons per output byte on the worker's log thread. With nothing registered a chunk passes through without a copy. A failed step's verdict excerpt is redacted from the raw stderr tail **before** it is decoded, cut to its last line and shortened, a multi-line value is also redacted line by line there, and a tail that filled its 64 KiB ring drops its first, possibly cut, line (P10D-2).
3. **Spool.** The redacted bytes become a frame — at most `MAX_LOG_FRAME_BYTES` (32 KiB) of one stream of one step, numbered by a per-attempt sequence from 1 with no skips — and are appended to `<data_dir>/spool/<attempt>/frames` **before** they are sent. The spool is synced every 32 writes and at the end; the first sync (or declared gap) of an attempt also syncs the directory entries that lead to its files (`spool/<attempt>` in `spool/`, and `spool/` in the data directory once per process), so what a sync promises survives a power cut. The spool is bounded three ways (`spool::SpoolSpace`):
   - **per attempt**, `MAX_SPOOL_BYTES` (256 MiB);
   - **per worker**, a quota on the bytes every attempt's spool holds together — `spool_quota_bytes`, default 4 GiB (16 attempts at their cap); a spool's bytes leave the quota when it closes;
   - **by free space**: the spool never writes the data directory's file system below a reserve — `spool_reserve_bytes`, default 1 GiB — so the workspace, caches, markers and the spool's own `declared` and end records keep room. Free space is probed with `statvfs` at most once per 16 MiB admitted (in between an append costs two atomic updates), and after a probe finds the reserve reached at most once a second, however much the step prints.

   Output refused by any bound is not written, and its sequence range is declared as a gap in the end marker rather than dropped in silence; the step keeps running, the job keeps its verdict, and once space returns output is stored again. The worker logs `spool_refused` when the attempt finishes, with the refused frames by cause (`cap`, `quota`, `low_space`, `failed_writes`, `gap_limit`). A log carries at most 256 gap ranges (what `LogEnd` can carry): the range that brings it to 255 stays open and takes the rest of the attempt's output, so a disk hovering at the reserve degrades to one final gap rather than a gap list the controller would refuse. Gaps survive a worker restart: a gap between stored frames is re-derived from the file on reopen, and a refused *tail* is recorded in `spool/<attempt>/declared` (the highest declared sequence, replaced atomically and synced when a range of refusals opens and at each spool sync), so the reopened spool declares it and never numbers a declared sequence again. A write that *fails* is treated the same — the sequence is spent, the torn tail is cut so later frames stay decodable, and the missing range is declared; every chunk spends its own sequence, so one failed write never silently swallows what follows. On reopen a tail that does not decode is cut when it is a torn record or all zeros — a power cut can persist the file's new size before its unsynced data — and reported corrupt otherwise.
4. **Send.** Frames leave from the spool's send cursor, never more than `MAX_UNACKED_LOG_FRAMES` (256) in flight per attempt: a slow controller costs the worker disk and 8 MiB of window, nothing more. A frame written while the cursor is caught up goes out straight from memory, encoded from the borrowed bytes; only a backlog is read back from the spool. A lost session drops the reporter; the next session rewinds the cursor to the last acknowledgement — at the byte offset that acknowledgement named, not by scanning the spool — and resends. A lost bulk connection (protocol 7) rewinds the same way before anything is sent on the fallback route, and a newly attached one waits for the frames still in flight on control, so no frame is acknowledged past without having been stored.
5. **Store.** The controller resolves the attempt's `(run, job)` once per session (`dispatch::attempt_log_scope` — any attempt this worker owns, held or released), then appends the frame into `logs/<run>/<job>/<attempt>/` in the same record encoding, in sequence. A repeat of a stored sequence (a resend) is accepted and not written twice. A sequence **jump is no longer refused**: the frame is stored and the skipped range is recorded as a hole, so a capped or lossy spool can still close — and a later retransmission inside a hole lands as a fill. Every change to the hole list is persisted (the `holes` sidecar) before the frame is acknowledged, so a controller restart forgets no hole, even one whose neighbours sit in sealed segments. The log is capped at `MAX_LOG_BYTES` (256 MiB).
6. **Acknowledge.** `LogAck { through }` is sent only after the frame is written **and `fdatasync`ed** — and, for the first frame of an attempt or of a new segment, after the directory entries leading to it are synced too (the attempt, job and run directories every time a writer opens, the `logs` directory's own entry when `LogStore::open` creates it), so an acknowledged frame's file cannot vanish with a power loss. A write cut short (`ENOSPC`, a file-size limit) or a failed sync is cut back off the segment and the writer is dropped, so the next frame reopens from disk and never lands behind a partial record. A transient controller fault is never answered `LogRefused` (which is permanent); the connection is closed and the worker resends. A writer for an attempt released without its log ending is closed at release rather than kept for the process's life. That is the durability boundary: what the acknowledgement covers is byte-for-byte what a reader sees, and only then does the worker's cursor (`spool/<attempt>/cursor`) move so a restart resends nothing that is already safe.
7. **Complete.** After the last step and the teardown, finalization flushes the redactor, syncs the spool, writes the end it will declare into the spool as an end record (synced, so a restart re-sends exactly that end), waits — bounded by `LOG_FLUSH_TIMEOUT` (60 s) — for every *stored* frame to be acknowledged (a refused tail was never sent and is not waited for), sends `LogEnd { last_seq, gaps }`, and **under protocol 5 keeps the spool until `LogEndAck`**: the controller sends it only after the `end` marker file is durable, so a lost session can no longer strand a log whose worker-side backup was already dropped (a retransmit is idempotent — `finish` of an already-ended log answers the same acknowledgement). Protocol 4 and earlier keep the send boundary: the spool drops once `LogEnd` is handed to the session. The controller refuses a `last_seq` below what it stored; a `last_seq` past it declares the never-stored tail as a gap. An attempt whose log could not be closed in time is `Failed(Publication)` if it would otherwise have passed; a failure keeps its own class.

The `end` marker file is what makes a log *complete* — an end record inside a segment without the marker is a finish interrupted mid-write, repaired on the next open. `sentinel admin logs --attempt att_… [--follow]` prints the frames to the matching stream, reports `log incomplete` without `--follow`, waits with it, and names the merged gaps. The API reads the same files with authorization (`?after=&limit=&wait=1&step=`).

## On the controller: segments, index and the end marker (D04)

Each attempt's log is a directory `logs/<run>/<job>/<attempt>/`:

```
seg-000000            # sealed segment, plain until compressed
seg-000001.z          # sealed segment, zlib (`SNLZ` format 1)
seg-000002            # the active segment — never compressed while open
index                 # sparse checkpoints (`SNLI` format 1)
end                   # the completeness marker (`SNLE`), atomic
```

- **Segments** hold the record stream and rotate at `SEGMENT_BYTES` (4 MiB). A sealed segment is queued to a single background compressor thread, which writes `seg-NNNNNN.z` (`SNLZ` | format | codec | zlib stream) and removes the plain file only after the compressed one is durable. The active segment stays plain so the next append is a `write` + `fdatasync` and nothing else.
- **The index** is a sidecar of 41-byte entries — `kind` | `seq` | `seg` | `step` | cumulative `lines` | cumulative `bytes` | wall-clock `ms` — checkpointed at each segment's first frame, every step change, and every 64 frontier frames, plus a seal record per closed segment. A tail by sequence seeks to the last checkpoint at or below `after` and decodes only from that segment; a `?step=` filter serves one step's frames from the same seek. Index entries are in sequence order — fills are never indexed, since a late-arriving sequence would break the seek.
- **Holes and gaps.** A jump records `(prev+1 ..= seq-1)` as a hole; a frame inside a hole fills it and narrows the range. The live hole list is mirrored in `holes` (`SNLH` | format u16 | count u32 | `(from, to)` u64 pairs, at most 4,096), replaced atomically (tmp, fsync, rename, directory fsync) whenever it changes — jumps and fills are rare, so the append path pays nothing otherwise — and read back on reopen, where only the holes of uncovered segments could be re-derived by decoding. A stream fragmenting past 4,096 holes is refused rather than growing the list. At `finish` the marker's gap list merges three sources — observed holes, the worker's declared gaps (a capped spool), and the `last_seq` tail that was never stored — so a gap can never be silent; more than `MAX_MARKER_GAPS` (1,024) ranges coalesce the closest pair. A read that starts mid-stream does not report decode-observed holes: with earlier segments skipped, a jump cannot be told apart from a fill that already landed — the marker's list applies to a finished log and the `holes` sidecar to a live one.
- **Recovery.** Reopen sweeps `*.tmp`, truncates each plain segment to its complete-record prefix (an all-zero tail — a power cut persisted the segment's size but not its unsynced data — ends the prefix like a torn record; other undecodable bytes are corruption), keeps index entries only through the last seal record (later ones are provisional and regenerated by re-decoding the segments they name), rebuilds a missing or undecodable index from the stream, re-queues sealed-but-uncompressed segments, and lands the marker when the stream carries an end record without one — including when the record sits inside the last *sealed* segment: a covered top segment whose marker is absent is decoded once (bounded by `SEGMENT_BYTES`) so a lost `end` file cannot silently reopen a finished log. A compressed segment with a leftover plain twin keeps the `.z`. While the log lives, the writer tracks the index's valid tail: a failed provisional append is truncated back so a torn entry never hides later seals — and if the repair itself fails the index is abandoned until reopen rebuilds it, which fails appends honestly rather than writing unindexed records.
- **Legacy.** Logs written before D04 live at `logs/<attempt>.log`; readers (`read_tail`, the API's `NotFound` fallback, `admin logs`) still serve them, streaming in the same bounded chunks as segmented reads.

## What the database records (D05)

The marker is the truth about the file; `attempts.log_state` is what makes it durable metadata (migration 26): `pending` while the attempt is live, `complete` once the controller's `LogEnd` handling has landed both the marker and the row — the protocol-5 `LogEndAck` goes out only after both — and `incomplete` when the job's terminal transition ran first: worker loss, an older protocol's send-boundary `LogEnd`, or the flush timeout. Terminal publication stamps the state in the same transaction that releases the attempt and decides dependents, so a successful result is never published ahead of its data status. `incomplete` still upgrades when a retransmitted end lands — log frames and `LogEnd` stay acceptable for an attempt the worker owns even after release, because late bytes only complete the evidence — and the column can never regress (the `attempt_update` trigger refuses `NEW < OLD`). A job that finished with a degraded log keeps its verdict and shows `incomplete`; `admin logs` and the API name the gaps.

## Bounds

| Where | Bound |
|---|---|
| frame | 32 KiB |
| in flight per attempt | 256 frames |
| worker spool per attempt | 256 MiB, then gaps |
| worker spools together | `spool_quota_bytes` (4 GiB), then gaps |
| data directory free space the spool leaves | `spool_reserve_bytes` (1 GiB), then gaps |
| gap ranges a spool declares | 256 (the last stays open) |
| controller log per attempt | 256 MiB, then refused |
| segment size | 4 MiB |
| index checkpoint cadence | 64 frontier frames |
| gap ranges on the wire | 256 |
| gap ranges in the end marker | 1,024, coalesced |
| finalization wait for acknowledgement | 60 s |

Memory on either side is the window plus one chunk; everything else is on disk. The worker reads its spool back in the same discipline — `send_next` reads only what `limit` records can occupy, and reopen/rewind scan in 64 KiB windows — so a full 256 MiB spool is never 256 MiB of RAM, and the legacy flat reader streams rather than loads. A process crash loses nothing the spool wrote; a power cut can take the frames written since the last sync. The reopened spool resumes the sequence after the last complete record — but never below a sequence the controller acknowledged (that frame is durable there even if the worker's unsynced copy is gone) nor below one it declared missing — so nothing the controller holds is ever re-numbered over. An acknowledgement past everything the spool numbered (the controller holds frames a power cut took from the worker) moves the spool's end past them, so the end it declares agrees with what the controller stored. Declared gaps survive the restart: those between stored frames are read back from the sequence jumps, and a refused tail from the small `declared` file, replaced atomically with each new gap range and at every sync. Acknowledged sequences are never declared as gaps. A spool reopened after a crash **without** its end record belongs to an attempt the crash cut short: whatever it printed after its last durable frame is unknown, so its end declares one more sequence — past everything stored, declared or acknowledged — as a gap, and the log cannot close as complete over the loss ([reconciliation](reconciliation.md)). A controller crash mid-write leaves a torn (or, after a power cut, zeroed) tail that is cut on the next open and re-sent by the worker, whose cursor never passed it; a segment tail that is neither is reported corrupt.

**Crash consistency, verified.** `crates/sentinel-worker/tests/crash_consistency.rs` drives one workload through the real code — the attempt marker; a capped spool with appends, syncs, acknowledgements, a refused range and its end; a log store with a jump and its fill, a finished log with declared gaps, a 130-frame log that seals and compresses a segment, a store reopen and a live log — and checks, after a crash at any point, that every promise made so far holds (acknowledged frames present byte-for-byte, synced spool frames present, declared gaps and ends unchanged, the cursor never ahead of an acknowledgement, the marker present) and that resending everything converges to the uninterrupted run. It crashes the workload two ways. `posix_crash_states` (`SENTINEL_CRASH_TESTS=1`, needs `strace`) records every file-system call of one run and replays it into a model that persists only what POSIX promises — file data only after that file's `fsync`/`fdatasync`, a directory entry only after its directory's — and checks five crash states after every call: only what was synced; every namespace change but only synced data; unsynced appends half-torn; unsynced growth zero-filled (the size persisted, the data not); everything issued. `power_cut_on_dm_flakey` (`SENTINEL_POWER_LOSS_TESTS=1`, as root, with `dm-flakey`) runs the workload on real ext4 and XFS over a `dm-flakey` device and cuts power after chosen operations by switching the device to `drop_writes` without freezing the file system, then unmounts, restores, mounts and checks what the disk kept. Journaling file systems persist more than the model (an ext4 `fsync` commits the whole journal), so the model is what catches a missing directory sync; the real devices catch what the model does not imagine — it was ext4 that showed a spool's size persisted without its data.

## Searching a log (O05)

`LogStore::search` scans frames past `after` in stored order with one precompiled `memmem` finder, at most `SEARCH_SCAN_BYTES` (4 MiB) of payload and `limit` matching lines per request, one report per line. A literal split across frames of one step and stream is found because the scanner keeps each stream's open line — whether it was already reported and its last `needle.len() − 1` bytes, in a fixed buffer — and joins those bytes with the next frame's first line (each side shorter than the needle, so any match there crosses). A request that stops at a bound returns that state with `next_after` as an opaque carry (format byte, `after`, needle length, per stream flags/step/bytes; at most 533 bytes, hex on the wire); the next request seeds from it and skips the frames at or below `after` outright, so the result is the same as one unbounded scan whatever the cut: a frame boundary, a sealed segment, another stream's frames or many megabytes in between. Without a carry the state is replayed from the segment holding `after` — the sealed segment itself when `after` is its last frame — by searching only each replayed frame's last line. A literal containing a newline is matched only within one frame.

## Writers, pages and retention

- **Writers.** An attempt's open writer (two descriptors and a frame of scratch) sits behind its own lock; the store-wide map lock only finds it, so one attempt's `fdatasync` never delays another's frame, and `has_end` from inside a writer transaction never waits on a flush. A finished log's writer closes at once. Attempts that never send `LogEnd` — a lost worker, a lapsed lease, a flush timeout — cannot hold theirs forever: at most `MAX_OPEN_WRITERS` (1,024) are open, the least recently used idle one closing first, and the maintenance pass closes writers idle for `WRITER_IDLE` (10 min). A later frame or end reopens the log from disk, which folds its state back exactly (holes included).
- **Pages.** The API reads through `read_page`: at most `limit` frames and `PAGE_BYTES` (1 MiB) of payload per page — JSON escaping keeps that under ~6 MiB on the wire, inside the CLI's 10 MiB body limit — and at most `PAGE_SCAN_BYTES` (16 MiB) decoded past `after`, so a step filter over a long log answers with `next_after` instead of decoding to the end. With a step filter the answer also knows whether the log has moved past that step (a later step's checkpoint follows the step's last one); a `wait=1` poll for a finished step answers at once. Parked polls wait on the log store's append notifier and re-read only when their own attempt's stored frontier moved, at most every 100 ms.
- **Newest window.** `read_newest` (`LogStore::newest_page`) returns the newest frames of one step (or of the log) under a page's frame and byte bounds, in stored order. The index gives the step's end (the first checkpoint of another step after its last checkpoint) and a start at the latest entry with a page of frames or bytes before it, so the head is never decoded; a rolling window keeps memory at one page while the decoder runs to the real end, frames past the last checkpoint included. `LogStore::last_seq` of a log with neither a live writer nor an end marker likewise decodes forward from the final index entry instead of answering with that checkpoint. The failure view ([diagnostics](diagnostics.md)) uses both.
- **Retention.** `sweep_expired` removes attempt directories whose newest byte (the `end` marker, `index` or top segment) is older than `log_retention`; open writers are skipped, and since idle writers close, an abandoned attempt's log falls under retention too. A pass examines at most 4,096 attempt directories in run-name order and the next pass resumes after the last run it finished.

Spool recovery after a worker restart is in [reconciliation](reconciliation.md): every leftover spool is delivered from its cursor and closed before the attempt is abandoned. The index's line and wall-time checkpoints are written but no reader seeks by them yet; tails seek by sequence and step.

## Verification

`crates/sentinel-protocol` (`logs::tests`): records round-trip; every prefix of a record is `Incomplete`, never misread; an oversized frame does not encode.

`crates/sentinel-worker` unit tests: the redactor replaces a value split across three chunks, emits a partial value at flush rather than swallowing it, prefers the longest match, ignores short values and carries per stream; the spool survives a reopen with a torn tail cut, keeps its cursor, resends only what is unacknowledged, continues the sequence and advances its send cursor in bounded reads — a capped spool refuses with declared gaps whose sequences stay spent — asserted after a reopen too, with and without the end persisted (`a_crash_after_a_refused_tail_still_declares_it`) — and scans over a multi-window spool stay bounded and in order (D07). Spool space (R01/D06): a disk at its reserve refuses output into one declared gap at one probe per back-off and stores output again once space returns, admitted appends probe once per grant, a closed spool leaves the quota (`a_disk_at_its_reserve_refuses_into_declared_gaps_and_recovers`); spools share the quota and get it back when one closes; the gap list stops at what `LogEnd` can carry with the last range taking the rest; the end record survives a reopen and a spool without one declares its cut tail; an acknowledgement past the spool moves its end; a zeroed tail is cut and garbage is still corrupt; the free-space probe reads the file system.

`crates/sentinel-worker/tests/spool_space.rs`: over real TLS into a real `LogStore`, a pipe whose quota runs out after three frames closes its log within seconds with the refused two declared as `(4, 5)` and counted as quota refusals — before the fix the end waited for an acknowledgement of the refused tail until the 60 s flush timeout. `tests/log_write_faults.rs` (`RLIMIT_FSIZE`, so the kernel accepts part of a write): a frame cut short is not acknowledged, its resend and the frames after it read back exactly after a reopen — before the fix the log reopened as `Corrupt("log record")` — and the spool declares the cut sequence and stays decodable. `tests/crash_consistency.rs` is described above.

`crates/sentinel-store/tests/logs.rs`: frames stored in sequence and acknowledged once durable, a resend acknowledged without a second copy, a jump stored with its hole and the fill landing, tail by sequence/limit/step, the end refused below the stored frontier and complete with merged gaps (observed, declared, truncated tail), nothing after the end and an idempotent re-finish, a torn segment tail and torn index tail cut on reopen, a zeroed segment tail cut while non-zero garbage stays corrupt, sealed segments compressing and reading identically across the boundary, the index's step/line/time checkpoints, holes and fills surviving a store reopen — in the active segment and in a sealed one (`a_hole_in_a_sealed_segment_survives_a_reopen`: the fill is stored after the restart, and a finish without it still declares it) — idle writers closed and capped with their logs then expiring, pages bounded in bytes and decode with `next_after` and a finished step reported, a pre-D04 flat file still reading, and the size cap completing through the gap list. `crates/sentinel-store/tests/log_search.rs`: a literal split across a sealed segment found once in one request, in paged requests resuming exactly at the seal (the 4 MiB budget stops there), without a carry, and across budgets of 128 and 37 frames — including a stderr line finished 400 frames later; the budget cut landing between every pair of frames of a two- and three-piece split, a reported line not reported again when its continuation crosses a cut, and every `after` without a carry giving the same answer; the 4 MiB bound as a hard bound over a 300-frame log, progress with a one-byte budget, and a carry refused for another `after`, another needle length, bad hex, truncation or trailing bytes.
