# API and CLI

Implemented in `crates/sentinel-api` (HTTP/1.1 on the crate's own bounded acceptor — 64 connections (at most 16 from one peer address, an IPv6 /64 counted as one; a configured reverse proxy is exempt), 8 handler permits of which at most 3 go to transfer bodies, and a separate budget of 32 parked long polls (at most 8 of one user, whatever credential it presents) that hold a connection but give their handler permit back while they wait, so 5 permits always serve everything else; deadline-bounded heads/bodies/writes) and `sentinel api …` (the CLI client in `crates/sentinel`). `sentinel server` opens the API on `api_listen` (default `127.0.0.1:7080`) beside the worker link. The human web interface is a separate process in front of it ([web interface](web-ui.md)).

## One surface

Everything a person, the CLI, the web interface or an agent does goes through the same routes, authenticated the same way and authorized by the same store predicates. The web interface is not a second path into the controller: it renders pages and relays updates by calling `/api/v1/…` with the browser's own session cookie ([web interface](web-ui.md)).

Plain HTTP on loopback by default; TLS and exposure are a reverse proxy's job. The session cookie is `__Host-sentinel_session` (Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict), which a browser accepts from `localhost` or over HTTPS only — deliberately.

A response body that fails mid-stream, or ends shorter than its declared length, closes the connection (`Request::respond` sets `close` and, on a failed write, shuts the socket) instead of reusing it for another request head. Otherwise the client would read the next response out of the middle of the unfinished body, a framing desync; keep that branch when changing `respond`.

## Authentication and authorization

| Credential | How | Mutations |
|---|---|---|
| `Authorization: Bearer sntl_…` ([API credentials](api-credentials.md)) | `tokens::authenticate`: one digest lookup; scope is a ceiling on the account's live membership | allowed; no CSRF (nothing sends a bearer ambiently) |
| session cookie ([local authentication](local-authentication.md)) | `local_auth::authenticate` | require the `x-sentinel-csrf` header with the session's CSRF secret, else `forbidden` |
| `Authorization: Bearer sntl_at_…` ([OAuth](oauth.md)) | `oauth::authenticate_access`: one statement (token, grant and account by key); the grant's scopes are the ceiling | allowed; no CSRF |

The credential yields a `Principal`; every route then asks the store — `auth::require_repo` for anything under a run, `auth::list_repos`, `tenancy::pools_for_tenant` and `workers::in_pool` under an `Authority` — so a resource the caller may not see is `not_found`, never revealed. A bearer credential can never step up. Every route also requires the **scope** in its row below: an OAuth access token carries exactly its grant's scopes, while a session or `sntl_` credential carries the scopes its permissions imply (`read` → every `:read` scope and `secrets:metadata`, `run` → `runs:write` and `cache:write`, the administrative bits one to one), so their authority is unchanged. A missing scope is `403 forbidden` with `details.scope` and `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer error="insufficient_scope", scope="…"`; every `401` from `/api/v1` carries `WWW-Authenticate: Bearer realm="sentinel", resource_metadata="…"` naming the RFC 9728 location of `{issuer}/api/v1` ([OAuth](oauth.md#access-tokens-on-the-api)), plus `error="invalid_token"` when a token was presented (the login and hook routes excepted). A credential the store is too busy to check is `rate_limited` (`429`, `details.retry_after_ms`), never `401`, so a client does not take overload for a dead credential. A session's idle deadline slides (one write per session per five minutes) as it is used.

| `GET /attempts/{id}/logs/search?q&after&limit&carry` | `read` | `logs:read` | bounded literal search: `q` is 1–256 bytes, percent-decoded (`+` is a space), matched byte for byte with `memmem`, one report per line. A string split across consecutive frames of the same step and stream is found too, once, and reported with the frame it ends in — whatever lies between the pieces: other frames, a sealed log segment, or the cut between two requests (the scan keeps each stream's open line as its last `len(q) − 1` bytes). Frames past `after` are scanned in stored order, at most 4 MiB of payload (a frame that would pass it waits for the next request) and `limit` matching lines (default 100, max 500; checked between frames) per request → `{attempt, matches:[{seq, step, stream, text}], next_after, next_carry, complete}`; `text` is the matching line, at most 512 bytes around the match (from the match itself when it began in an earlier frame). When a bound stopped the request, `next_after` and `next_carry` (opaque hex, at most 1,066 characters) are where it resumes: pass them back as `after` and `carry` with the same `q`. A `carry` for another `after` or `q` length, or malformed, is `invalid_request`. Without `carry` the state is rebuilt from the segment holding `after` (the sealed one when `after` ends it), so a line begun before that segment can be missed or repeated — older clients only. `complete` means the log is finished and was scanned to its end |
| `GET /attempts/{id}/failure?budget&limit&after\|cursor` | `read` | `logs:read` | bounded failure context → `{schema:"sentinel.failure/1", authoritative, failed_step, timings_ns, cache, reports, artifact_reports, tail, truncated, omitted_diagnostics, log_complete, log_gaps, next_cursor}`. The authoritative job state is returned only while this attempt still owns the job: its fence is the job's, the job is leased under it and not queued. From the moment of a rerun (before any new lease) or a lapsed offer, the older attempt reports `current_attempt:false` with no job state. With no cursor, Go test/rustc/cargo JSON parsing starts at sequence 0 and reads at most 4 MiB or 1,024 frames; when that page is cut, the failed step's newest window (at most 4 MiB/1,024 frames, sought through the sparse index so a later `always()` step cannot displace it) is parsed too and is the excerpt's source. Each log report carries `window` (first/last frame sequence parsed). Legacy flat logs have no sparse index, so their excerpt begins at the available scan position and pages forward. With `artifacts:read`, report files the attempt published as artifacts are parsed as `advisory` reports with an `artifact` `{name, path}` ([diagnostics](diagnostics.md#report-files)); without it `artifact_reports.status` is `scope_required`. A parser report carries frame evidence references and SHA-256 provenance; it is `incomplete` unless its selected stream was completely covered; an invalid diagnostic is dropped and counted in `parse.malformed_records`, never the whole report. Failures and errors are budgeted before warnings and informational records. Text defaults to 8 KiB, `budget` accepts 1–65,536, and `limit` defaults to 20 diagnostics (max 20). With no `budget` the whole compact JSON body is at most 8 KiB: tail frames go first, oldest first, and a frame larger than the remaining excess keeps its newest bytes and carries `cut:true`; then diagnostic messages longer than 256 bytes are shortened from the end, last diagnostic first, keeping at least their first 256 bytes and carrying `message_cut:true`; whole diagnostics are dropped (and counted in `omitted_diagnostics`) only after that. Control characters other than newline and tab become U+FFFD in excerpts and diagnostic messages alike; a run of binary frames is one `{binary:true, sequence, end_sequence, bytes}` entry that spends no text budget; report text is untrusted data |

Every secret route checks the path, query, headers and body shape first, then resolves the tenant slug through the caller's membership and the repository name inside the same transaction that authorizes the operation, so a foreign tenant or repository answers exactly like a missing one. `repo` is percent-decoded (`repo=Acme%2Fapp`). A reused `Idempotency-Key` with a different request is `idempotency_mismatch` (422), distinct from a stale `If-Match` (`conflict`, 409). The retry record stores a keyed MAC of the request under a subkey of the master key, never a digest that could be recomputed from the database.

```sh
sentinel api --token-file ~/.sentinel/token status run_…
sentinel api --token-file ~/.sentinel/token logs att_… --follow
sentinel api --token-file ~/.sentinel/token cancel --run run_…
sentinel api --token-file ~/.sentinel/token rerun job_…
sentinel api --token-file ~/.sentinel/token workers --tenant acme --json
sentinel api --token-file ~/.sentinel/token queue --tenant acme --limit 50
sentinel api --token-file ~/.sentinel/token drain wrk_…
sentinel api --token-file ~/.sentinel/token undrain wrk_…
```

`--json` prints the server's document, and a failure as one `sentinel.error/1` line on stderr; text output is for people. `--token-file`/`--token`/`SENTINEL_TOKEN` accept an `sntl_` credential or an `sntl_at_` access token. Exit codes are the shared table in [CLI](cli.md#exit-codes): 0 success, 1 remote fault or malformed answer, 2 usage, 3 `unauthenticated`/`forbidden`, 4 `not_found`, 5 `conflict`/`idempotency_mismatch`, 6 busy or unreachable after retries. `--token-file` keeps the secret out of the process list; `--token` and `SENTINEL_TOKEN` exist for tooling that already protects its environment.

Draining needs a platform-admin credential on the API; on the controller's own host the same change is `sentinel admin worker drain --id wrk_… --data-dir …` (and `undrain`), which acts on the store directly like the rest of `sentinel admin`.

## The web interface

The controller serves no page of its own besides the OAuth consent and device pages: `GET /` is `not_found`. The web interface is `sentinel-web` ([web interface](web-ui.md)), a client of these routes.

## Client keys behind a reverse proxy

The per-client rate limits on the unauthenticated routes, the sign-in lockout pair `(client, account)` and the public-body slot all use one **client key**:

| Peer | Key |
|---|---|
| Not in `trusted_proxies` | its own address (an IPv6 /64 counts as one); every header is ignored |
| In `trusted_proxies` (loopback when the key is absent), `X-Forwarded-For` ends in a valid address | that last address; a proxy appends the real peer, so the entry before it is whatever the client wrote and is never read |
| In `trusted_proxies`, no `X-Forwarded-For` or a last entry that is not an address | the proxy's **connection**: its address and source port |

The last row is the dependency on the proxy. A proxy that does not send `X-Forwarded-For` used to give every client behind it one key: one guesser exhausted the allowance of all, and five wrong passwords for an account from the shared key locked that account out for the owner. Now each upstream connection is a client of its own, so a guesser throttles and locks out the connections it uses, not the people behind other ones. That is the finest identity the controller has without the header, and it is weaker than a real client address: a client that reaches the controller through a new upstream connection for every request (a proxy without keep-alive) gets a fresh key each time. The following still bound it, and none depends on the header: the global ceiling of every budget, the 8 handler and 4 public-body slots, the 10 s body deadline, and the account-wide lock, which locks the account for everybody after four times the per-client failures (`ACCOUNT_LOCK_FACTOR`) whatever address they come from. Unauthenticated per-client limits, the lockout and the per-client body slot are therefore only fully effective when the proxy overwrites or appends `X-Forwarded-For` with the address it saw, as the web interface does with `NUXT_CLIENT_IP_HEADER` ([web interface](web-ui.md#client-addresses)). A proxy must be listed in `trusted_proxies` for its header to be read, and nothing else may reach the API port: a client that reaches it from a trusted address could name any client.

Quotas that reconnecting must not mint are still keyed on the proxy's address alone, so everything behind a proxy that names no client shares them: the dynamic client registrations per registrant ([OAuth](oauth.md)). A trusted-proxy peer is exempt from the per-peer connection cap, which is why a proxy that names no client is not capped per connection.

## Mail limits

Every message the controller queues (an address verification or an email invitation) is bounded per **recipient address**, whoever asked for it, on top of the per-account and per-client limits: at most **5 messages to one address per hour** and **at least 60 seconds between two messages of the same kind** to it. A request refused by this allowance sends nothing and leaves the link already mailed valid (the link is minted only when the message can be queued). The bounds are shared by every requester, so they cap the mail a stranger can make the deployment send to a victim, but they also mean a stranger can spend a victim's allowance: while it is spent, the holder's own resend waits (at most until the oldest message leaves the hour; `POST /me/email/resend` answers `429`, every unauthenticated or address-taking route still answers `202`), and an operator can still mint a link on the host with `sentinel admin account email-link` ([admission](admission.md#email-identity-and-email-invitations)). An invitation is not delayed by a verification message, or the reverse; both count toward the hourly 5. Delivered messages keep counting for seven days of outbox retention, so delivery does not reset the allowance. Email-registered accounts that never verify their address are retired after seven days ([admission](admission.md#email-identity-and-email-invitations)).

## Unauthenticated bodies

A handler permit is never held while a client with no credential yet sends its body. The login, the sign-up, setup and public email routes, both webhooks (`/hooks/github`, the generic hook) and the OAuth forms and registration read their whole body first: if it is not already in the connection's buffer, the handler permit is given back, the read waits for one of 4 body slots (`sentinel_api::PUBLIC_BODIES`) and must finish within 10 seconds, slot wait included (`rate_limited`, or `503 temporarily_unavailable` on the OAuth routes, when no slot frees up in time), and only then is a handler permit taken again. Each client (the admission key: peer address, or the `X-Forwarded-For` address behind a trusted proxy, or the proxy's connection when it names none; [client keys](#client-keys-behind-a-reverse-proxy)) may hold only one of those slots at a time, and a second body from the same client is turned away at once, so one host cannot fill the pool and block other people's sign-in or webhook delivery. A client that trickles a body therefore delays only its own other unauthenticated bodies, and never a run read, a token refresh, a health check or another client's sign-in. A trusted-proxy peer is exempt from the per-peer connection cap, so the clients it forwards are bounded in the slots instead: together they may hold at most 3 of the 4 (`sentinel_api::PROXY_BODIES`), each for at most 5 seconds, slot wait included (`sentinel_api::PROXY_BODY_TIME`), and one slot is left to direct clients however many addresses the proxy names; a forwarded reader that finds its share spent waits for it within those 5 seconds and is then turned away (`rate_limited`, or `503` on the OAuth routes). Many distinct forwarded addresses can still take the 3 for five seconds each; that is bounded by the admission budgets, not by this pool. Authenticated routes keep the 120 s body deadline: their sender has already presented a credential. Bodies are also bounded in size (login 16 KiB, OAuth forms and registration by their own limits, webhooks by the intake limits), and `Content-Length` and chunk sizes are plain digits (decimal or hexadecimal); a sign or any other character is `400`.

## Live updates and long polls

`GET /runs/{id}/wait` and `GET /attempts/{id}/logs?wait=1` are the live paths every client uses — the CLI's `wait` and `--follow`, MCP, and the web interface's event streams. A parked poll:

- takes one of 32 subscriber slots (8 per user), holds its connection, and gives its handler permit back while it waits, so parked polls never starve requests doing work (`sentinel_api::SUBSCRIBERS`, `SUBSCRIBERS_PER_USER`, `RESERVED_HANDLERS`);
- re-authorizes whenever the tenant's authorization epoch moves (a membership, role or grant change, a suspension) and then answers what a fresh request would (`not_found`), rather than keep serving a reader who lost access until its deadline;
- ends as soon as its client hangs up (a non-blocking `peek` every 250 ms), giving the slot back, so a closed tab or an aborted upstream request never holds a slot to the 25 s deadline.
